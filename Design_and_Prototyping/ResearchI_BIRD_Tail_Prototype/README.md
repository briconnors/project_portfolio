## Research I:   BIRD Tail Prototype
*Designed and fabricated a lightweight tail for Professor Rosen's flapping-wing micro air vehicle under strict mass constraints.*

After studying avian and airplane mechanics, I developed theoretical force balance models in Excel to bound the required stiffness, geometry, and operating angle, while limiting the additional moment applied to the fuselage due to the weight.

Material behavior ultimately governed the design. I tested various configurations using soft carbon fiber and binder based composites, which proved durable but too flexible to withstand the dynamic loading. I then evaluated carbon fiber rods of varying diameters (0.3, 0.5, 0.8, and 1 mm), observing tradeoffs between stiffness, flexibility, and failure modes. Thin rods experienced brittle fracture easily during handling, while thicker rods bent more but split longitudinally during fabrication; therefore, 0.5 mm rods offered the ideal balance between qualities. Additionally, I compared film materials to act as the lifting surface, ultimately selecting Kapton tape due to its low mass, ease of bonding, and consistent behavior during assembly. 

The final tail design met structural and aerodynamic requirements with a total weight of 0.31 grams.
