## Data-Driven Problem-Solving: Exploratory Analysis
*I performed  a comprehensive analysis of the NBER-CES Manufacturing Industry Database to examine relationships between wages, employment, and investment across U.S. Manufacturing sectors.*

After cleaning and visualizing the dataset, I initially identified a strong positive correlation between total payroll and production wages (r=0.95) however, there were weaker correlations between payroll and total employment (0.69) or production employment (0.6). This indicates that payroll growth outpaces workforce expansion, and rising payroll is driven more by an increase in pay levels than by hiring. 

The data was grouped by year to examine trends across the period 1960-2020, revealing that production worker employment remained relatively stable across years, while total payroll increased significantly over time. This divergence indicates that payroll growth was driven by rising wages per worker rather than workforce expansion, suggesting increasing wage concentration within parts of the manufacturing sector. 

The second portion of analysis examined the annual financial costs, gains, and capital composition. Based upon the correlation coefficient, equipment is closely related to net gain in profit (r=.94) while structures are less direct (.752), therefore resources should be put into means of production rather than expanding infrastructure.
